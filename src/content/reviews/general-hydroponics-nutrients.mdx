**General Hydroponics FloraSeries is a three-component base nutrient system: FloraMicro, FloraGro and FloraBloom, used together.** Add FloraMicro to fresh water first, stir, then FloraGro, then FloraBloom, and never pour one concentrate into another. The manufacturer's Light feed chart starts seedlings at **1.8 mL of each part per gallon (EC 0.4–0.5)** and rises to **6.0, 5.6 and 4.2 mL per gallon (EC 1.4–1.7)** in late growth. Keep the solution at **pH 5.5–6.5**, below **75 °F (24 °C)**, and change it every **7–10 days**. It works for home growers, and a University of Nevada, Reno greenhouse study grew lettuce and bok choy on it. The catch is that the chart is written for flowering crops on light schedules, so for lettuce, herbs and tomatoes, pick the row whose EC matches your crop. The table below does that for you.

## What is in each bottle

Each part has a different job, and none of them is a complete feed alone.

| Part | N-P-K | What it adds | Alone? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FloraMicro | 5-0-1 | Nitrogen, calcium, chelated micronutrients (boron, cobalt, copper, iron, manganese, molybdenum, zinc) | No |
| FloraGro | 2-1-6 | Nitrogen (mostly nitrate), potassium, magnesium 0.5% | Not stated |
| FloraBloom | 0-5-4 | Phosphorus, potassium, magnesium 1.5%, sulfur 1.0% | No: 0% nitrogen |

The calcium is the reason for three bottles. Penn State and Missouri Extension both explain that calcium reacts with phosphates and sulfates to form solids that roots cannot use. That is why concentrated hydroponic nutrients keep calcium apart from phosphorus and sulfur. FloraMicro carries the calcium; FloraBloom carries the phosphorus and sulfur. Once both are diluted in a full reservoir, they stay in solution.

General Hydroponics says plainly that FloraBloom has no nitrogen and would cause "severe nitrogen starvation" on its own. If a plant is yellowing while you feed "bloom" only, that is the first thing to check.

## Mixing order, and where General Hydroponics contradicts itself

![Six steps: fresh water first, then FloraMicro, FloraGro and FloraBloom one at a time with stirring, then check EC and adjust pH to 5.5–6.5 last; never mix two concentrates](/images/guides/gh-floraseries-mixing-order.svg)

*The FloraSeries program page order: FloraMicro always goes into the water first, then FloraGro, then FloraBloom. Check EC before you touch pH.*

Two General Hydroponics pages give slightly different orders after FloraMicro:

| Page | Order after water |
| --- | --- |
| FloraSeries 3-Part Feed Program | FloraMicro, FloraGro, FloraBloom |
| FloraGro product page (when adding supplements) | Silica first, calcium products and FloraMicro, then FloraBloom, then FloraGro |

Both agree on what matters: FloraMicro first, one part at a time, fully stirred, and never two concentrates together. For the plain three-part mix, follow the program page (Micro, Gro, Bloom). If you add a silica product, the FloraGro page puts it in before everything else.

## Which chart row to use for your crop

The FloraSeries chart runs four growth weeks at 18 hours of light and nine bloom weeks at 12 hours. It sorts feed strength by irrigation frequency, pot size and planting density. None of that describes lettuce in a Kratky jar or basil in a deep water culture bucket. What every row does include is an EC range, and university guides give EC ranges by crop. So match the two.

![Range bars for Light, Medium and Aggressive feed EC at each stage: seedlings 0.4–0.8, early growth 0.9–2.1, late growth 1.4–2.5, early bloom 1.4–2.4, late bloom 0.9–1.6, ripen 0.6–1.1; a shaded band marks lettuce at 1.2–1.8](/images/guides/gh-floraseries-ec-by-week.svg)

*EC targets from the General Hydroponics weekly chart. The Light feed's growth weeks 3 and 4 fall inside Oklahoma State's lettuce range of 1.2–1.8.*

| Crop (EC range) | FloraSeries row | Micro / Gro / Bloom (mL/gal) | Row EC |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Basil (1.0–1.6) | Light, growth week 2 to 3 | 3.6 / 3.4 / 2.6 to 4.9 / 4.6 / 3.4 | 0.9–1.4 |
| Lettuce (1.2–1.8) | Light, growth week 3 to 4 | 4.9 / 4.6 / 3.4 to 6.0 / 5.6 / 4.2 | 1.2–1.7 |
| Pepper (0.8–1.8) | Light growth, then Light early bloom | 5.3 / 4.6 / 6.0 at bloom | 1.4–1.7 |
| Cucumber (1.7–2.0) | Medium, early bloom | 6.1 / 5.3 / 6.8 | 1.6–1.9 |
| Spinach (1.8–2.3) | Medium, late growth | 6.8 / 6.4 / 4.8 | 1.6–2.0 |
| Tomato (2.0–4.0) | Aggressive, early bloom | 7.6 / 6.6 / 8.5 | 2.0–2.4 |

Two points to keep in mind. Leafy greens and herbs never flower on purpose, so they stay on the growth rows. And there is a cross-check: the Light feed's growth weeks list 95–150 ppm of total nitrogen, which sits inside Penn State's 100–150 ppm nitrogen for lettuce.

For tomatoes, the University of Tennessee's fruiting-crop guide gives EC 0.5–1.0 for seedlings, 1.0–1.5 at transplanting and 2.0–3.0 for much of the season. That runs from the Light seedling row through Medium growth rows to the Aggressive bloom row. Oklahoma State's tomato range (2.0–4.0) goes higher than any FloraSeries row; the Aggressive chart tops out at EC 2.5.

## How strong to mix: what the one university study found

The University of Nevada, Reno grew Rex butterhead lettuce, Nevada Summer Crisp lettuce and bok choy in greenhouse float culture on the General Hydroponics Flora series (Ferguson, Saliga and Omaye, 2014).

| Trial | Strengths compared | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cycle 1 | Flora at 1:1:1 to 300, 400 and 500 ppm total dissolved solids | Fresh weight similar at all three levels |
| Cycle 2 | 400 ppm vs 1200 ppm (the study's "manufacturer recommended" level) | Bok choy 384.7 g vs 619.1 g; Summer Crisp 133.1 g vs 197.1 g; butterhead 131.3 g vs 150.9 g |

So weak solutions grew acceptable greens, but the full-strength solution grew bigger heads, most of all for bok choy. An organic solution in the same trial yielded less than even the low Flora level. The paper does not state which ppm scale its meter used, so these ppm figures cannot be turned into EC. For a home grower, the practical reading is to start leafy greens at the Light rows and move toward the top of your crop's EC range if growth is slow. General Hydroponics' usage guide also says to use less concentrated solutions when growing conditions are more intense (hot, bright or fast-drying).

## Step-by-step: a 5-gallon batch for lettuce

1. **Test your water.** If it reads 200 ppm or more of dissolved salts, General Hydroponics says to get an analysis. Above 70 ppm calcium, use Hardwater FloraMicro. Missouri Extension suggests source water EC of 0.2–0.8, ideally below 1.0.
2. **Fill the reservoir** with 5 gallons (about 19 L) of water below 75 °F (24 °C).
3. **Shake each bottle.** Use a separate syringe or measuring cup for each part, or rinse between them.
4. **Add FloraMicro.** At Light growth week 3 (4.9 mL/gal), that is 24.5 mL, about 5 teaspoons. Stir well.
5. **Add FloraGro:** 4.6 mL/gal is 23 mL. Stir.
6. **Add FloraBloom:** 3.4 mL/gal is 17 mL. Stir. (These three amounts are HydroRigor's arithmetic from the chart rate times 5 gallons.)
7. **Check EC.** Expect about 1.2–1.4 plus whatever your water started with. Oklahoma State advises letting the reading settle for a couple of minutes.
8. **Adjust pH last** to 5.5–6.5. Oklahoma State's advice is to adjust pH only after EC is right, adding pH down slowly and waiting several minutes between doses.
9. **Top off with plain water** as the level drops, and **replace the whole solution every 7–10 days**.

To scale any chart row to another reservoir size, the [nutrient mixing calculator](/tools/nutrient-mixing-calculator/) multiplies each part separately. Enter the three rates one at a time; it does not pick a FloraSeries row or predict EC. For crop targets, use the [pH and EC targets tool](/tools/ph-ec-targets/).

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cloudy or white solids after mixing | Two concentrates met before dilution, or parts added too fast | Remake the batch; add each part to the full water volume and stir between |
| EC far above the chart row | Source water already high, or a mL/L rate used as mL/gal | Measure plain water first; check the chart says per gallon |
| EC below the chart row | Under-dosed or one part skipped | Add the missing part in order, then re-check |
| New growth yellowing on a "bloom only" feed | FloraBloom has no nitrogen | Use all three parts at the chart ratio |
| Scale or deposits with hard tap water | Water calcium above 70 ppm | Switch to Hardwater FloraMicro, or use RO or rainwater |
| Leaf edges burning in hot, bright weather | Solution too strong for the conditions | Drop to the Light row or dilute with water |
| pH keeps drifting | Adjusted before EC was set, or old solution | Set EC first, then pH; change the solution on schedule |

## FAQ

**Can I use just FloraGro and FloraMicro for lettuce?**
General Hydroponics' charts use all three parts in every growth week, including leafy-green rows. FloraBloom is the part that brings phosphorus and sulfur, so leaving it out changes the balance the chart was built on. Use all three.

**Can FloraBloom be used alone during flowering?**
No. It contains no nitrogen, and General Hydroponics warns it would cause severe nitrogen starvation used alone.

**What ppm scale does the chart use?**
The ppm column on the FloraSeries chart is the 500 scale. If your meter uses a different scale, compare EC in mS/cm instead; EC is the same on every meter.

**Should I follow the "Flush" week?**
The chart lists water only in the last bloom week. HydroRigor found no university source on flushing hydroponic vegetables, so this page takes no position.

**How long does a bottle last?**
General Hydroponics says unopened bottles are stable for about 2 years and opened bottles should be used within one growing season. Keep them sealed, upright and out of direct sun.

**Is FloraSeries good for beginners?**
Yes, if you own an EC meter and do not mind measuring three parts. If you want the fewest steps, a one-part nutrient is simpler.

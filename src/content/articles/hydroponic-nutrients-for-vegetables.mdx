There is no single feeding schedule for “hydroponic vegetables.” Use a complete program documented for the crop and system, account for source water, scale only the current product-specific dose, and monitor both the solution and plant. Leafy and fruiting vegetables differ in duration, structure, and nutrient demand.

## Group vegetables by crop behavior

Leafy crops such as lettuce have a comparatively short cycle and compact structure. Fruiting crops such as tomatoes and peppers remain in the system longer, develop larger root systems, require support, and change through vegetative, flowering, and fruiting stages.

Virginia Cooperative Extension demonstrates that crop structure affects system fit. The same logic applies to feeding: do not use a crop name alone without stage and production context.

## Begin with essential elements and complete instructions

Penn State Extension lists essential macro- and micronutrients and explains that deficiencies or excesses can appear quickly in hydroponic systems. Confirm that the whole program supplies required elements. A front-label N-P-K ratio does not prove completeness.

If the program uses several parts, treat each as a separate measured component and follow the documented order. Do not improvise a supplement because two products use similar names.

## Include the water and root medium

University of Missouri Extension advises source-water analysis for pH, EC, alkalinity, and individual elements. In inert media, reservoir and root-zone readings may be similar; organic media can interact with the solution and change root-zone conditions.

Record whether the system recirculates. In a recirculating reservoir, plant uptake and water loss can alter composition over time. Adding concentrate to restore an EC reading does not prove the original nutrient ratios have been restored.

## Use measurements as evidence, not recipes

EC measures combined ionic conductivity. pH affects nutrient availability. Neither identifies every element. Calibrate instruments and measure consistently, then interpret the readings with roots, foliage, water level, environment, and recent actions.

For accepted source-specific crop records, use the [pH and EC target reference](/tools/ph-ec-targets/). Do not average ranges across unrelated crop stages or systems.

## Scale the label carefully

Use the manufacturer’s current rate for the exact formulation and crop context. HydroRigor’s [nutrient mixing calculator](/tools/nutrient-mixing-calculator/) can scale a liquid mL-per-liter or mL-per-US-gallon label dose to reservoir volume. Calculate multi-part products separately. The output is concentrate volume—not a predicted EC or crop recommendation.

## Keep a feeding record

Record product and formulation, water source, reservoir volume, dose for each component, date, stage, measurements, water additions, solution changes, and plant observations. When something changes, prefer one documented adjustment at a time.

NFT can grow strawberries when a shallow, continuous nutrient stream reaches roots while crowns and fruit remain properly supported. The difficult parts are not the acronym: they are channel capacity, root mass, uniform flow, drainage, pump interruption, and matching irrigation to a changing fruiting crop.

## The NFT water path

Virginia Cooperative Extension describes NFT as a recirculating system: solution leaves a reservoir, enters growing channels, travels past roots, and returns for reuse. The channel slope, inlet, outlet, pump, and return line form one system. Changing any one changes the flow seen by plants.

University of Missouri Extension notes that NFT commonly supports lettuce, herbs, and strawberries. It also warns that leaks, clogs, or pump failure can cause plants to wilt quickly. Build access to inspect the first and last planting positions rather than assuming uniform delivery.

## Fit strawberries to the channel

Strawberry roots and crowns need different conditions. Support the crown above persistent solution while allowing roots to enter the channel. Do not force a large root plug into a narrow opening that restricts drainage or damages the crown.

Plan channel volume for mature roots. Roots can slow flow or collect debris. The return must handle the pump’s delivery without backing solution around crowns.

## Establish plants before demanding fruit

Use healthy planting material and let roots establish contact with the moist channel. Cornell’s propagation work shows the importance of preparing runner plants for soilless systems. A home setup should likewise treat transplant quality and establishment as a separate phase.

During establishment, inspect each root zone rather than compensating for one dry plant by flooding every channel.

## Flow is observed, not assumed

Check that solution reaches all channels, returns freely, and does not pond. A timer or rated pump value cannot prove actual flow through a channel containing roots. Mark normal reservoir level, listen for changes, and plan an immediate response to pump interruption.

The goal is a thin, reliable stream with roots also exposed to air—not a pipe filled like DWC. Avoid copying a numerical flow target without the channel geometry and crop context that produced it.

## Fruiting changes demand

Illinois Extension observations note that delivery may need adjustment with plant growth, fruit set, temperature, and sunlight. Record those conditions with any change. A hotter, brighter day and a larger canopy can change water use without proving that nutrient concentration should be increased.

Use the manufacturer’s current directions for the nutrient product. See HydroRigor’s [pH and EC target reference](/tools/ph-ec-targets/) for the accepted source-specific strawberry record; this article does not create a second range.

## Daily and weekly checks

Daily, confirm pump operation, inlet flow, return flow, reservoir level, crown condition, and any wilt. Weekly, inspect channels and roots for obstruction, review records, and perform the cleaning or measurement maintenance required by the system and instruments.

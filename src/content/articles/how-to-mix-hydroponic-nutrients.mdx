Mix hydroponic nutrients by starting with the intended water volume, measuring each product separately, adding components in the manufacturer’s order, and mixing thoroughly between additions. Never combine concentrated parts directly unless the manufacturer explicitly instructs it.

## 1. Verify the exact program

Use the current label or technical chart for the exact formulation. Confirm crop or stage, required parts, dose unit, water assumptions, and whether the directions create a final solution or a stock concentrate.

Do not copy a rate from a product with similar N-P-K numbers. Formulations can differ in micronutrients, density, concentration, and required companion products.

## 2. Prepare the water and tools

Measure the actual final reservoir volume. Use clean, material-compatible containers and dedicated measuring tools. If source-water chemistry matters to the program, use current analysis rather than assuming tap, well, and purified water are interchangeable.

University of Missouri Extension recommends testing source water for pH, EC, alkalinity, and individual elements.

## 3. Calculate each component separately

For a liquid dose stated in mL per liter or mL per US gallon, multiply the normalized dose by reservoir volume. HydroRigor’s [nutrient mixing calculator](/tools/nutrient-mixing-calculator/) performs that volume scaling with an exact gallon-to-liter conversion.

The calculator does not choose the label rate, handle grams, formulate nutrients, or predict final EC. For multi-part programs, run a separate calculation for each part.

## 4. Add one product at a time

Add the first component to water and mix until dispersed or dissolved as instructed. Then add the next component to the diluted solution. Penn State Extension explains that concentrated calcium can react with concentrated phosphates or sulfates, which is why some programs use separate stock tanks.

If the manufacturer specifies a mixing order, follow it. Do not pour concentrates into the same measuring cup.

## 5. Allow the solution to stabilize

Mix for the documented time and confirm dry products have dissolved. Do not increase temperature or agitation beyond product guidance merely to force a concentration that exceeds solubility.

Inspect for sediment or clouding and verify that the reservoir circulation or aeration is operating as designed.

## 6. Measure with maintained instruments

Measure EC and pH only after mixing. Calibrate, rinse, and store probes according to their instructions. Oklahoma State Extension describes calibration against known buffer or conductivity standards and consistent measurement timing.

Remember that EC is not an elemental analysis. A matching number does not prove the correct product or component ratio was used.

## 7. Label and record

Record date, water source, final volume, product name, formulation, dose for each part, stage, measurements, and operator notes. Clearly label any stock concentrate with identity and concentration, and keep it away from children, pets, and food preparation.

**Kratky hydroponics** is a way to grow plants in still nutrient solution with **no pump and no electricity**. You fill a container once, hang a net pot so its bottom **½–1 inch (1.3–2.5 cm)** touches the solution, and leave it. As the plant drinks, the level falls and a moist **air gap** opens under the lid; the roots in that gap take in oxygen, so no air pump is needed. Plan on **1–2 gallons (about 4–8 L) of solution per lettuce plant** for the whole crop, mixed to **EC about 1.5 mS/cm** and **pH 5.5–6.5**. Lettuce is ready about **4–6 weeks after transplanting**. The one rule that matters: **don't top it back up**.

The method is named after B.A. Kratky of the University of Hawai‘i, whose publications are the main sources here, together with a University of Florida (UF/IFAS) fact sheet on the 5-gallon bucket version. HydroRigor has not grown a crop for this guide; every figure is tied to a source listed at the end.

## How the Kratky method works

In most hydroponic systems a pump moves solution past the roots or bubbles air through it. Kratky's method does neither. In his words the tanks are filled with nutrient solution "prior to planting", and nothing is added afterward.

At planting, only the bottom of the net pot touches the solution. Capillary action wets the growing medium and the seedling starts to drink. As the level falls, the space between the lid and the solution fills with moist air. The roots that grow in that space are what Kratky calls **oxygen roots**: they take in air while the lower roots keep taking up water and nutrients.

![Three cross-sections of a Kratky container over time. Day 1: the tank is filled once and the bottom half inch to 1 inch of the net pot sits in the solution. Mid-crop: the level has fallen and a moist air gap has formed where oxygen roots grow; do not top up. Harvest, 4 to 6 weeks after transplanting: the level is well down and the lower roots still reach the solution.](/images/guides/kratky-air-gap-over-time.svg)

*The falling solution level creates the air gap. Starting depth and harvest timing from Kratky's VC-1 (University of Hawai‘i, 2010).*

This is also why the method has one firm rule. Oxygen roots that have grown in air can drown if the level rises over them. Kratky's tank guide says never to raise the solution level, and his 2004 paper says that raising it more than 2 cm (0.8 in) after the air gap forms may injure plants.

## How much solution per plant

Because nothing is added later, the container must hold the whole crop's water from day one. The sources agree closely:

- **UF/IFAS:** each lettuce plant needs 1–1.5 gallons of water from seed to harvest. Its 5-gallon bucket holds three 2-inch net cups.
- **Kratky, VC-1:** tanks get 1.5–2 gallons per plant before planting, so a 50-head tank needs about 75–100 gallons. The method suits crops that need less than 2 gallons per plant for the whole season.
- **Kratky, 2004:** a 200-gram lettuce plant in a 4-liter bottle uses about 3–4 L, and the level usually drops 15–25 cm (6–10 in).
- **Kratky, Acta Hort. 843:** 3–6 L used per plant in lettuce trials.

*Solution per lettuce plant in four sources. UF/IFAS HS1488; Kratky VC-1 (2010), Kratky (2004) and Kratky, Acta Horticulturae 843.*

**Our recommendation: plan on 1.5 gallons (about 5.7 L) per lettuce plant.** That is the top of the UF/IFAS range and the bottom of Kratky's tank range, and three plants at 1.5 gallons fit a 5-gallon bucket. The ranges differ because plant size and weather differ; a larger head in warm weather drinks more.

## What to grow

Kratky's VC-1 lists leafy, romaine and semi-head lettuce, cilantro, green onions, kai choy, pak choi and watercress. These are short crops that finish before a modest tank runs dry.

Long-season crops are a poor fit. VC-1 says crops such as tomatoes and cucumbers are not suited to this tank method, and UF/IFAS says fruiting crops do better in other methods. Kratky's 2004 paper adds that the bottle version suits only short crops like lettuce. For tomatoes, see our [hydroponic tomatoes guide](/crops/hydroponic-tomatoes/), which uses drip-fed buckets.

Basil and other herbs are often grown this way at home, but none of the sources used here gives Kratky figures for them beyond cilantro and green onions, so this guide gives none. Our [hydroponic basil guide](/crops/hydroponic-basil/) covers what university trials did measure.

Keep light off the solution. Kratky's bottle paper says to cover or darken the bottle to discourage algae, so choose an opaque bucket or wrap a clear container.

## Step-by-step setup

1. **Start seedlings.** Both UF/IFAS and Kratky transplant lettuce seedlings at 1–3 weeks old. Our [lettuce seed guide](/crops/hydroponic-lettuce-seeds/) covers germination.
2. **Size the container.** Allow 1.5 gallons per lettuce plant, or 1–2 gallons if you are following one source exactly.
3. **Mix the solution.** Aim for EC 1.5 mS/cm. Kratky runs 1.2–1.5 in hot weather and 1.6–2.0 in cool weather, and warns to be wary above 2.0 and avoid going over 3.0. In his bottle paper, 5 g of a 10-8-22 hydroponic fertilizer in 4 L (about 1 gal) gives EC 1.5. If your fertilizer comes in two parts, keep them separate until each goes into the water. See [how to mix hydroponic nutrients](/nutrients-water/how-to-mix-hydroponic-nutrients/) or the [nutrient mixing calculator](/tools/nutrient-mixing-calculator/).
4. **Check pH.** Kratky's range is 5.5–6.5; UF/IFAS uses 5.5–6. Below 4.0 or above 7.0 is abnormal and can stunt or kill plants (VC-1). The [pH and EC target reference](/tools/ph-ec-targets/) lists lettuce targets by source.
5. **Fill to the right height.** The lower ½–1 inch of each net pot should sit in the solution (VC-1); in a 4-liter bottle Kratky starts with the lower 3 cm (1.2 in) submerged. UF/IFAS leaves about 1 inch of air space in its bucket.
6. **Set the seedlings in the pots and fit the lid.** Then leave the lid on. VC-1 says not to lift the cover during the crop.
7. **Give it light and a mild temperature.** Lettuce grows best at 60–70 °F (16–21 °C) and tolerates the 80s and low 90s °F (VC-1). For light, University of Kentucky gives lettuce a daily light integral of 12–17 mol/m²/day. Our guide to [indoor hydroponic systems for home](/systems/indoor-hydroponic-systems-for-home/) covers grow lights.
8. **Harvest.** Leafy and semi-head lettuce are ready about 4–6 weeks after transplanting, longer in winter (VC-1). UF/IFAS gives about 3–4 weeks after transplanting for its bucket, and Kratky's bottle lettuce was ready about 6 weeks from seeding.

Our [DIY hydroponic system guide](/systems/diy-hydroponic-system-for-beginners/) has a parts list for the bucket build, and the [hydroponic lettuce guide](/crops/hydroponic-lettuce/) covers varieties, tipburn and bolting.

## Why you must not top it up

A new grower's instinct is to refill the bucket when the level drops. In a Kratky system the dropping level is the design working. Refilling covers the oxygen roots, and Kratky warns that submerged roots can drown.

If the container really will run dry before harvest, VC-1 allows a small rescue: add about **½–1 inch** of water or half-strength solution, no more. If that happens every crop, the fix is a deeper container, not more refills. Outdoors, rain entering a tank does the same damage by raising the level, so keep rain out.

## Troubleshooting a Kratky grow

| Problem | Likely cause | What to do | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Plants wilt after a refill | Oxygen roots submerged | Don't raise the level; in an emergency add only ½–1 in | Kratky VC-1 |
| Container nearly dry before harvest | Too little solution per plant | Add ½–1 in of water or half-strength solution; use a deeper container next time | Kratky VC-1 |
| Seedling dries out in the first days | Pot bottom not touching the solution | Start with the lower ½–1 in of the pot submerged | Kratky VC-1 |
| Green solution or slime | Light reaching the solution | Cover or darken the container | Kratky 2004 |
| Stunted or dying plants | pH below 4.0 or above 7.0, or EC over 3.0 | Check both; remix within range | Kratky VC-1 |
| Lettuce seed won't sprout | Heat: seed can go dormant from 82 °F (28 °C) | Germinate somewhere cooler | Kratky VC-1 |
| Mosquitoes in the tank | Open water under the cover | Screen the area and put window screen in the tank below the starting solution line | Kratky VC-1 |
| Level rises outdoors | Rain entering the tank | Shelter the container | Kratky VC-1 |

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the Kratky method need an air pump?

No. The moist air gap that forms as the level falls supplies oxygen to the upper roots, so there is no pump and no electricity (UF/IFAS HS1488; Kratky).

### How much water does a Kratky lettuce plant need?

About 1–2 gallons for the whole crop: 1–1.5 gallons per plant in the UF/IFAS bucket and 1.5–2 gallons per plant in Kratky's tanks. Kratky measured about 3–4 L for a 200-gram head.

### Can I top up a Kratky container?

Not as a routine. Raising the level can drown the oxygen roots. If it will run dry before harvest, add only about ½–1 inch of water or half-strength solution (Kratky VC-1).

### How long does Kratky lettuce take?

About 4–6 weeks after transplanting 1–3-week-old seedlings (Kratky VC-1); UF/IFAS gives about 3–4 weeks after transplanting for its bucket. From seed in a bottle, Kratky's lettuce took about 6 weeks.

### Can I grow tomatoes with the Kratky method?

The tank and bottle methods described here are not suited to long-season crops such as tomatoes and cucumbers (Kratky VC-1), and UF/IFAS says fruiting crops do better in other methods. This guide gives no tomato volumes because its sources give none.

### What EC and pH should a Kratky solution have?

EC about 1.5 mS/cm (1.2–1.5 in hot weather, 1.6–2.0 in cool weather) and pH 5.5–6.5, according to Kratky. UF/IFAS uses about 1.25 mS/cm and pH 5.5–6.

### Is Kratky or deep water culture better?

Kratky is simpler and needs no power, which suits a first grow of a few lettuce plants. Deep water culture adds an air pump and suits more plants and repeated crops; Oregon State notes it needs effective aeration. Our [lettuce systems comparison](/crops/hydroponic-lettuce-systems/) sets the two side by side.

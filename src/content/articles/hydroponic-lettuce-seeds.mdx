Choose hydroponic lettuce seed by matching lettuce type and cultivar traits to the actual indoor environment. Start with growth habit, mature head size, temperature and day-length adaptation, disease-resistance information, and the harvest form you want. “Hydroponic” on a packet is not enough.

## Choose the lettuce type first

UF/IFAS Extension describes several lettuce groups used in protected culture, including butterhead, loose-leaf, romaine, and crisphead types. They differ in canopy shape, density, and market form. Those differences affect spacing, airflow, and how easily inner leaves can be inspected.

For a compact first crop, a loose-leaf or small butterhead type may fit more systems than a dense, large head—but that is a use-case choice, not a guarantee of performance.

## Match the cultivar to the environment

UF/IFAS advises home growers to select lettuce adapted to their available day length and warmer conditions. Indoor rooms can also be warmer than expected because lights, pumps, and limited airflow add heat.

Read the breeder or seed supplier description for bolting tolerance, leaf type, mature size, and resistance codes. A resistance claim applies to named pathogens and does not mean the plant is generally disease-proof.

## Buy seed with traceable identity

Look for the cultivar name, lot information, germination information, and a supplier that identifies handling and storage conditions. Avoid a generic “lettuce mix” when the goal is to compare system performance; mixed types make timing and response harder to interpret.

Do not assume seed labeled organic makes the harvested crop certified organic. Organic representation is governed by production and certification requirements, not seed wording alone.

## Run a small, labeled trial

Sow a manageable number of seeds in clean propagation material. Label the cultivar and sowing date. Record emergence consistency, transplant quality, canopy size, bolting, leaf quality, and harvest timing under the actual system.

If two varieties are compared, keep the system, spacing, light, and management as consistent as practical. That does not turn a home observation into a universal trial, but it makes the next local decision more informative.

## Avoid false precision

Germination and harvest times printed on a packet are context-dependent. Temperature, seed age, light, and growing conditions change outcomes. Use the figures as planning inputs, not promises.

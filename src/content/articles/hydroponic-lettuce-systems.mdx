DWC, NFT, and Kratky-style passive reservoirs can all support lettuce, but they manage water and root oxygen differently. Choose DWC for an inspectable immersed root zone with active aeration, NFT for multiple plants with dependable circulation, or passive culture when reducing moving parts matters more than active control.

## Quick comparison

| Decision | DWC | NFT | Passive reservoir |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Water contact | Roots remain in a deeper solution | Shallow solution moves past roots | Solution level falls as plants use water |
| Active equipment | Usually an air pump | Water pump and return path | None for solution movement |
| Main failure concern | Loss of aeration or degraded root zone | Interrupted flow, clog, or leak | Incorrect level, inadequate root-air zone, or exhausted solution |
| Practical scale | One container to rafts | Rows or channels | Small independent containers |
| Best fit | Grower can monitor aeration and reservoir | Grower can monitor continuous flow | Grower understands the passive method and wants few moving parts |

## DWC: simple structure, active oxygen

Missouri Extension describes DWC as comparatively simple to establish. Lettuce is supported above a reservoir, roots remain immersed, and aeration supplies oxygen. It is easy to observe in a small container, but the air system and solution still need attention.

## NFT: efficient layout, tighter interruption tolerance

In NFT, a shallow stream moves through sloped channels and returns to the reservoir. UF/IFAS describes NFT among small-system options, and Missouri Extension notes its common use for lettuce. It can organize many plants compactly, but a failed pump or blocked channel can affect roots quickly.

## Kratky-style passive culture: no pump, not no management

In a passive reservoir, the solution level declines and part of the root system occupies the developing air space. The container must exclude light and support the plant while preserving the intended root-zone relationship. It is easiest to reason about at small scale with a crop whose cycle and demand fit the available solution.

## Other decisions do not disappear

All three methods still require a suitable cultivar, propagation, light, canopy space, water context, complete nutrition, and sanitation. System choice cannot compensate for a lettuce variety poorly matched to the environment.

The general [lettuce growing guide](/crops/hydroponic-lettuce/) connects those crop decisions. For stored source-specific nutrient ranges, use the [pH and EC reference](/tools/ph-ec-targets/) rather than assigning one value to every system.

## A conservative beginner choice

Choose the method whose failure mode you can see and respond to. If power reliability is poor, NFT needs a clear contingency. If continuous aeration is not practical, active DWC is a poor fit. If the passive root-air mechanism is unclear, do not treat “no pump” as sufficient instruction.

There is no absolute winner; the best fit changes with scale, power, crop duration, and observation routine.

For indoor hydroponics, choose tomato seed by growth habit and mature size before flavor, color, or novelty. A compact dwarf or determinate cultivar may fit limited height more easily; an indeterminate cultivar needs continuing vertical support and pruning access.

## Understand growth habit

UF/IFAS Extension distinguishes determinate tomatoes, which have a more bounded growth pattern, from indeterminate tomatoes that continue producing along elongating stems. Dwarf cultivars add another compact option. These categories shape trellis height, spacing, pruning, and crop duration.

Utah State University Extension specifically advises smaller tomato varieties for indoor growing because root and shoot space are constrained. “Indoor” does not mean a full-size vine becomes compact.

## Match seed traits to the system

Check mature height and spread, fruit size, days-to-maturity context, and whether the cultivar was described for greenhouse, protected culture, containers, or field production. A hydroponic root zone does not remove the plant’s structural needs.

For a media bucket, confirm that the support plan can carry the selected habit. For a compact integrated garden, compare the maximum light height with the mature canopy and leave space between leaves and the fixture.

## Read resistance codes precisely

Disease-resistance codes refer to named pathogens or races; they are not a general immunity claim. Verify the breeder’s legend and keep the packet or technical sheet. Resistance can reduce one risk while sanitation, environment, and plant handling remain necessary.

## Do not overread variety lists

Extension publications often name cultivars that performed or were commonly used in a particular location and production period. UF/IFAS explicitly places its cultivar discussion in north Florida field and protected-culture contexts. Treat named examples as evidence from that context, not an automatic recommendation for every indoor room.

Run a small comparison under your actual system. Keep labels and record germination, transplant quality, mature structure, flowering, support demand, and fruiting. Do not call a local observation a universal result.

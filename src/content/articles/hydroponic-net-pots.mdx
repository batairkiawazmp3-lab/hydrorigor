**For lettuce, herbs and other leafy greens, use 2-inch (5 cm) net pots; for bigger plants, step up to 3-inch (7.5 cm) pots, and for a single tomato in a 5-gallon bucket, a 6-inch (15 cm) pot.** Those are the sizes extension builds use: UF/IFAS puts three 2-inch net cups in a 5-gallon Kratky bucket, Kentucky State says 2-inch pots are typical for lettuce, Oklahoma State's tower uses 3-inch pots, and UF/IFAS Volusia County sets a 6-inch pot in the lid of a one-plant tomato bucket.

Set each pot so only its bottom touches the solution. In a floating raft, UF/IFAS HS943 wants the cup's bottom level with the underside of the foam, never more than **1/16 inch** below it. In Kratky tanks, Kratky lowers the bottom **½–1 inch (1.3–2.5 cm)** of the pot into the solution at transplanting. Cut the hole so the pot's lip rests on the lid (Minnesota), space leafy greens about **8–12 inches (20–30 cm)** apart, and soak used pots in a **10% bleach solution** before you reuse them (Kentucky State).

HydroRigor has not tested net pots; every figure below comes from a university, extension or peer-reviewed source listed at the end.

## What a net pot does

Minnesota Extension's definition is the simplest: a net pot is **a pot with holes or slits in the sides** so the root system can reach the nutrient solution below. The pot holds the seedling and its starting plug in place in a lid, raft, channel or tower, while the roots grow out through the mesh into water or a film of solution.

Two rules follow from Minnesota's guide: drill holes **just small enough for the lip of the net pot to rest on top**, and keep the pot only **partly submerged** so young roots get air. In Kratky's University of Hawai'i work, the upper roots become "oxygen roots" in the moist air gap as the solution drops (CTAHR VC-1).

Net pots do not need to be bought. UF/IFAS HS943 allows **styrofoam coffee cups with slits cut in the bottom** in place of commercial net pots, and UC Master Gardeners in Orange County hand out Kratky starter kits built from nut jars, milk jugs and yogurt containers with a single net cup. Our [DIY hydroponic system guide](/systems/diy-hydroponic-system-for-beginners/) has full builds.

## Which net pot size for which plant

No source gives a full size chart, so this table collects the sizes the extension builds actually use:

| Size | Typical use | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 in (5 cm) | Lettuce and leafy greens in Kratky buckets, tanks and rafts | UF/IFAS HS1488; Kentucky State; UF/IFAS HS943 |
| 3 in (7.5 cm) | Vertical towers; floating rafts | Oklahoma State HLA-6724; UF/IFAS HS943 |
| 6 in (15 cm) | One tomato, pepper or cucumber in a 5-gal DWC bucket | UF/IFAS Volusia County |

**Our recommendation:** 2-inch pots for any leafy crop, the size in three separate extension builds; 3-inch pots when the system is built for them, such as a tower; a large pot like Volusia County's 6-inch one for a fruiting plant in its own bucket. See our [DWC guide](/systems/deep-water-culture-hydroponics/) and [Dutch bucket guide](/systems/dutch-bucket-hydroponics/) for the rest of those builds.

The pot must fit the plug. Kentucky State notes a 50-cell tray makes 2-inch plugs; for tomatoes, Tennessee W 844-C recommends a seed cube of **at least 1.5 × 1.5 inches** (about 4 × 4 cm), so check the cube fits the pot's opening before you sow.

## Hole sizes: the conflict between sources

Net pots taper, so the right hole is smaller than the pot's nominal size, and the sources do not agree on how much smaller:

| Pot | Hole saw | Foam thickness | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 in | 1¾ in | 1½ in | UF/IFAS HS943 |
| 2 in | 2 in | At least ½ in | Kentucky State |
| 3 in | 2½ in | 1½ in | UF/IFAS HS943 |
| 3 in | Cut to a template with a jigsaw (no size given) | 5 × 5 in vinyl post | Oklahoma State HLA-6724 |

The difference makes sense once you look at what each build wants. UF/IFAS HS943 sets the hole so the **bottom of the cup ends level with the underside** of a thick 1½-inch raft; a tighter hole holds the pot higher. Kentucky State's Kratky tank uses a thinner board and wants the pot's bottom third in the solution, so a hole at the full 2 inches, which lets the pot drop lower, fits that build. That reading is ours; neither source explains its hole size.

Pot shapes vary between makers, and no source gives a hole size for a specific product, so follow Minnesota's rule: the lip rests on the lid and the pot can't fall through. **Drill one test hole in a scrap of your lid or board first**, set a pot in it and check the depth before drilling the rest.

## How deep the pot sits in the solution

This is the part UF/IFAS HS943 calls **the most critical aspect** of a floating garden: the depth of the net pot or root ball in the solution. The figures differ by system:

- **Floating raft (UF/IFAS HS943):** the cup's bottom level with the foam's underside, no lower than **1/16 inch** below it; the root mass then wicks up water. Float the raft on at least **5 inches (13 cm)** of solution.
- **Kratky tank (CTAHR VC-1):** lower the bottom **½–1 inch** of each pot into the solution at transplanting; then let the level fall.
- **Kratky bottle (Kratky 2004):** lower **3 cm (about 1¼ in)** of a 7.5 cm pot into the solution.
- **Kentucky State tank:** the bottom **third** of the pot in the solution.
- **Kratky bucket (UF/IFAS HS1488):** leave about **1 inch (2.5 cm)** of air space.
- **Once roots grow out (Minnesota):** keep roots only **1/3 to 1/2 submerged** in a Kratky system.

The common thread: the plug needs moisture from below at first, and the upper roots need air later. In Kratky, set the depth at transplanting and don't top the level back up: Kratky warns that raising the level more than **2 cm (about ¾ in)** after the air gap forms may injure plants (Kratky 2004). Our [Kratky guide](/systems/kratky-hydroponics/) walks through the full method.

## What to put in a net pot

Usually, only the plug. UF/IFAS HS943 says transplants should be **fully rooted in a soilless medium** and that you should **not pack additional media around the root ball** inside the cup. Virginia Tech lists rockwool, foam or peat/coir cubes as the starting plugs for NFT, and Kentucky State starts its Kratky seedlings in rockwool or Oasis cubes.

Larger pots in towers and buckets are different. Oklahoma State's tower fills **expanded clay balls** around each plant so it stands up in a 3-inch pot. If you fill a pot, Minnesota says to soak any substrate in water for **24 hours** and change the water before transplanting. Our [growing media guide](/equipment/hydroponic-growing-media/) compares clay, rockwool, coir and perlite and notes that no source sets a fill medium for DWC or Kratky net pots.

## Spacing the holes

Spacing is set by the crop, not the pot. The sources give:

| System | Spacing | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Floating raft, most plants | 12 in apart, 6 in from the edges | UF/IFAS HS943 |
| Kratky tank, lettuce | 48 or 60 plants per 4 × 8 ft tank (1.5 or 1.9 per sq ft) | Kentucky State |
| DWC raft, lettuce | 3–4 plants per sq ft | Kentucky CCD-CP-63 |
| NFT channel, leafy greens | 8 in on center | Virginia Tech SPES-463 |
| Tower, thin-leaf lettuce | 6–8 in | Oklahoma State HLA-6724 |
| Tower, head lettuce, kale, spinach, strawberry | 12 in | Oklahoma State HLA-6724 |

Raft density varies widely: Kentucky State's 1.5–1.9 plants per square foot sits below Kentucky CCD-CP-63's 3–4. Kentucky State's figures are for a no-pump Kratky tank; CCD-CP-63 is written for controlled-environment lettuce production. Neither source explains the gap, so start at the lower density in a Kratky tank. In a tower, Oklahoma State also says to **stagger the holes** so pots do not touch inside the pipe.

## How to set up net pots, step by step

These steps follow the sources above. They are a starting point, not a tested HydroRigor method.

1. **Choose the size** from the table above: 2-inch for leafy greens, 3-inch for towers, a large pot for one fruiting plant per bucket.
2. **Use an opaque, food-safe lid or raft.** Minnesota asks for food-safe materials; Oklahoma State and Minnesota say to cover the reservoir to keep light out and limit algae.
3. **Mark the holes** at the crop's spacing, at least 6 inches (15 cm) from the edges of a raft (UF/IFAS HS943).
4. **Drill one test hole** with the hole saw for your pot (1¾ inch for a 2-inch pot in 1½-inch foam, UF/IFAS HS943) and check that the lip rests on the surface.
5. **Start seedlings in plugs** that fit the pot; transplant when the roots show at the plug's sides. UF/IFAS HS1488 transplants Kratky lettuce after **1–3 weeks**.
6. **Set the depth**: cup bottom level with the raft's underside in a floating raft, or the lower ½–1 inch in the solution in a Kratky tank (CTAHR VC-1).
7. **Check pH and EC** of the solution; UF/IFAS HS1488 gives **pH 5.5–6** and about **1,250 µS/cm** for Kratky lettuce. The [pH and EC targets tool](/tools/ph-ec-targets/) has other crops.
8. **After harvest, clean the pots** as in the next section before reusing them.

## Cleaning and reusing net pots

Plastic net pots can be reused, but they need cleaning first, and one source advises against reuse at all:

| Step | Instruction | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Loosen old roots | Leave roots to decay in the pots for 1–2 weeks | Kentucky State |
| Wash | Brush off debris; wash in soapy water and rinse | Iowa State; UC Master Gardeners |
| Disinfect | Soak in 1 part bleach to 9 parts water for at least 10 minutes | Iowa State; UC Master Gardeners |
| Bleach | Unscented household bleach, 5–9% sodium hypochlorite | UC Master Gardeners |
| Mix fresh | Use within 2 hours (Iowa State) or 24 hours (UC Master Gardeners) | Iowa State; UC Master Gardeners |
| Alternative | Wipe with undiluted 70% isopropyl alcohol | Iowa State |
| Finish | Rinse and dry before reuse | Kentucky State; Iowa State |

Kentucky State soaks net pots in **10-percent bleach**, then rinses and dries them; Minnesota says to sanitize net pots, foam boards and anything else that touched the water between plantings.

The sources differ on how long mixed bleach stays useful: Iowa State says **two hours**, UC Master Gardeners **24 hours**.

Oregon State's bucket guide is more cautious: it advises home growers to **avoid recycling and reusing materials**, which it calls the primary cause of disease spread and pest problems in home systems. Our reading: cleaned, bleached plastic pots are fine to reuse after a healthy crop, but after root rot or another root disease, replace the pots.

## Troubleshooting net pots

| Problem | Likely cause | Fix | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pot falls through the hole | Hole too big for the pot's lip | Drill a smaller hole; test on scrap first | Minnesota; UF/IFAS HS943 |
| Seedling wilts after transplanting | Pot sits too high; plug can't reach the solution | Lower the pot so its bottom touches the solution | UF/IFAS HS943; CTAHR VC-1 |
| Plants stall after topping up a Kratky tank | Air roots drowned by raising the level | Don't raise the level more than 2 cm after the air gap forms | Kratky 2004; CTAHR VC-1 |
| Plug stays soggy, roots brown | Too much media packed in, or pot too deep | Use the plug alone; reset the depth | UF/IFAS HS943; Minnesota |
| Green algae around the pots | Light reaching the solution | Use an opaque lid; cover any gaps | Minnesota; Oklahoma State |
| Old roots stuck in the mesh | Fresh roots cling to the slots | Let roots decay 1–2 weeks, then scrub | Kentucky State |
| Disease in the next crop | Reused pots not disinfected | 1:9 bleach soak for 10 minutes; replace after root disease | Iowa State; Oregon State EM 9456 |

## Frequently asked questions

### What size net pot is best for lettuce?

2 inches (5 cm). UF/IFAS puts three 2-inch cups in a 5-gallon Kratky bucket, and Kentucky State says 2-inch pots are typical for lettuce.

### What size hole saw do I need for 2-inch net pots?

UF/IFAS HS943 uses a 1¾-inch hole saw in 1½-inch foam; Kentucky State uses a 2-inch bit in thinner board. Test one hole: the lip should rest on the lid (Minnesota).

### How far should a net pot sit in the water?

Only its bottom. In a floating raft, the cup's bottom is level with the raft's underside (UF/IFAS HS943); in Kratky, the lower ½–1 inch is in the solution at transplanting (CTAHR VC-1).

### Do I need growing media in a net pot?

Not with a rooted plug. UF/IFAS HS943 says not to pack extra media around the root ball. Larger pots in towers can be filled with clay pebbles (Oklahoma State).

### Can I reuse net pots?

Yes, after cleaning. Soak them in 1 part bleach to 9 parts water for at least 10 minutes, then rinse and dry (Iowa State; Kentucky State). Oregon State advises against reusing materials in home systems, so replace pots after a disease problem.

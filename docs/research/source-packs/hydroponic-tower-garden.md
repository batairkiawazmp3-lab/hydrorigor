## Figures and locators

**Oklahoma State HLA-6724 (February 2021)**
- How it works (Vertical Hydroponics section): gravity-fed; "nutrient-rich water is fed from
  the top and collected at the bottom".
- Advantages (same section): high-density yield per unit area; suits small sunny places such as
  balconies, patios and rooftops; year-round production inside; "more than 90 percent
  efficiency in water use"; no soil-borne diseases. No disadvantages are listed.
- Tower Garden section: commercial systems "can cost around $500 or more"; one tower holds
  28 plants; two towers fit in a 5 ft x 5 ft space (56 plants at one time); indoors, lights
  must be provided above the tower; crops listed: strawberry, lettuce, Swiss chard, herbs,
  spinach, kale, broccoli.
- Materials: vinyl fence post 5 in x 5 in x 8 ft; 3-inch PVC pipe (10 ft); 45-gallon tank;
  submersible pump rated 400 gallons per hour; light timer (used as the pump timer); 28
  three-inch net pots; about 10 holes in the top to spread solution.
- Steps: recommended tower height 5–6 ft; stagger the holes on each side so pots do not touch
  inside the tower; water splashing out of a pot can wet leaves and lead to fungal disease.
- Watering (step 19): lettuce can be watered continuously, 24 hours; strawberries seven to 10
  times a day for one hour each.
- Table 1, spacing on the tower (inches): strawberry 12; thin leaf lettuce 6–8; head lettuce 12;
  Swiss chard 12–14; spinach 12; kale 12; broccoli 18.
- Post preparation care: clean the tower with bleach after each crop; monitor pH and EC by crop
  (it gives no numbers and refers to HLA-6722); keep the tank water level up.
- Not given: light intensity or hours, pH/EC numbers, yields by weight.

**UF/IFAS St. Lucie County blog (Rezazadeh, 2025-02-06)**
- A pump moves water from a reservoir to the top; it trickles down past the roots; closed loop
  recirculates water.
- Crops listed: lettuce, spinach, kale, basil, cilantro, strawberries, cherry tomatoes.
- Gives no figures (no water %, yields, light or pH). Use only for the crop list and the
  description of how water moves.

**Touliatos et al. 2016 (abstract)**
- In the vertical system (ZipGrow towers), light (PPFD) and shoot fresh weight "decreased
  significantly ... from top to base"; the vertical system "produced more crop per unit of
  growing floor area" than horizontal hydroponics. The repository abstract gives no fold
  change or PPFD values; do not quote numbers.
- Use for: lower plants on a tower get less light and grow less; turn or light the tower
  evenly indoors.

**Wortman et al. 2016 (ASHS release)**
- Strawberries in vertical hydroponic towers in a high tunnel over two years at two Illinois
  sites; yield was highest in perlite mixed with coco coir or vermiculite with synthetic
  fertilizer; organic nutrient source gave about 15% lower yield; best cultivars varied by
  site and year ('Florida Radiance', 'Monterey', 'Evie 2', 'Portola', 'Seascape' each topped at
  least one site-year). Authors call for research on differences between tower levels.

**Registered sources to reuse**
- pH/EC targets by crop: HLA-6722 (`osu-ph-ec-guide`), lettuce EC about 1.5 and pH 5.5–6.0,
  DLI 12–17 (`uky-hydroponic-lettuce`), strawberries EC at or below 1.0 dS/m, pH 5.5–6.0
  (`ua-strawberry-irrigation`).
- Pump failure: in channel systems plants can wilt quickly, so power and pump backups matter;
  vertical levels add shading (`osu-hydro-hints-nft`, EM 9457).

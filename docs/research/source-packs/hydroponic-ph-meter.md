## Figures and locators

**UMass (Cox)**
- "Calibration is a must to get any useful information from pens and meters!"
- pH pens: calibrate with pH 7 buffer, or two-point with pH 4 and pH 7 buffers.
- Early on, calibrate at the beginning of every testing session until you learn how much the
  readings drift between sessions; readings drift over time even on expensive meters.
- Discard used calibration solutions.
- Pens and bench meters work on the same principles and should give the same readings when
  properly calibrated.
- Prices (2016): single pH or EC pens $50–60; combination pH/EC pen about $80. Only quote with
  the year, or leave out.

**Michigan State (Wollaeger, 2016)**
- Buffers used: pH 4.01 and 7.01.
- EC standard: 1.41 mS/cm is about 1413 µS/cm (meter and packet may use different units).
- pH changes with temperature: check the solution temperature before calibrating.
- Rinse the electrode with distilled water before and between calibrations.
- A probe left unused will likely dry out: soak it in storage solution for at least an hour,
  then calibrate.
- Never dip the probe into the large bottle of standard (it becomes inaccurate over time); pour
  some out, or use single-use sachets. Check standards are not expired.

**Purdue (Camberato and Lopez, 2009)**
- Portable, affordable units are available and very accurate.
- Units with automatic calibration and temperature compensation are desirable.
- Calibrate to the pH range you are testing.
- Store instruments and solutions at room temperature in a low-humidity room; never store
  solutions or meters in the greenhouse or outside.

## Figures and locators

**Solis-Toapanta, Fisher and Gómez 2020 (HortScience; small home-style systems)**
- Abstract/Materials: four plants in 7.6-L (about 2-gallon) aerated deep-water-culture units;
  178 mg/L N from a complete nutrient solution; seedlings transplanted 22 days after sowing;
  grown 8 weeks; pinched at weeks 4 and 6.
- Environments: indoor room at a constant 21 °C, 65% RH and DLI 9 mol/m²/day; greenhouse at
  29/24 °C day/night with DLI averaging 26.1 mol/m²/day. Greenhouse plants grew faster.
- Final shoot fresh mass: greenhouse 1,417 g (solution replaced) and 1,452 g (topped up, not
  replaced); indoors 344 g and 359 g. Solution management did not affect growth.
- Solution: pH adjusted to 5.5–6.5 after fertilizer additions. Indoors, EC under
  "top up without replacing" rose to 5.5 dS/m by week 6 without reducing growth over 8 weeks.
- Literature cited in the paper: basil tolerates EC 0.5–4.0 dS/m; commercial optimum often
  cited as 0.5–1.6 dS/m; commercial guidelines replace solution every 1–2 weeks; leaves can be
  harvested as early as 6 weeks after sowing.
- Authors' advice: frequent replacement may be unnecessary for indoor gardeners; if topping up
  without replacing, reduce fertilizer inputs indoors.

**Walters, Tarr and Lopez 2023 (PLoS ONE)**
- Sweet basil 'Nufar': optimal mean daily temperature for fresh mass rose from 32.6 to 35.5 °C
  as DLI increased from 6 to 11 mol/m²/day; branch number increased as DLI rose from 5.5 to
  13.2 mol/m²/day; plants reached marketable size about three weeks after transplant.
- Cites Walters and Currey for base temperatures of about 11 °C (10.9–12.1 °C across cultivars;
  11.3 °C for 'Nufar'): little growth below that.

**Walters 2015 (Iowa State research, Greenhouse Grower)**
- Deep-flow hydroponics; EC 1.6 mS/cm, pH 6, water 72 °F, 16-hour photoperiod.
- Sweet basil spacings 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 inches (9.3, 4.1, 2.3, 1.4, 1.0 plants per sq ft) at DLI
  about 6 and 15 mol/m²/day; higher DLI added 0.3–1.6 oz per sq ft; spacing did not change
  individual plant weight.
- Recommendation: for single-harvest basil under lights, 4-inch spacing; likely too dense for
  repeated harvests.
